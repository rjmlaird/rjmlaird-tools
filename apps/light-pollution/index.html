Estimate local sky brightness and Bortle class from a UK location, with practical guidance for visual observing.

Where will you observe?

A full postcode (LE1 6ST), a postcode district (LE1), a town or city from the list that appears as you type, or latitude and longitude in decimal degrees.

Where the glow comes from

    A modelled estimate, not a measurement. Treat it as accurate to about one Bortle class.

    The Bortle scale

    Sky brightness (SQM) is in magnitudes per square arcsecond; a higher number is a darker sky.

    How this is estimated

    The tool adds up the artificial light from about 200 towns and cities within 250 km. Each one contributes in proportion to its population (to the power 0.88) and falls off with distance (to the power −2.5), the shape of Walker’s law for city skyglow. The total is compared with a natural zenith sky of 21.9 mag/arcsec² and converted to a Bortle class and a naked-eye limiting magnitude.

    SQM = 21.9 − 2.5 · log₁₀(1.05 + Σ 0.01 · P0.88 · deff−2.5)

    The population exponent and the softening of distance within a town were fitted to about twenty approximate UK sky-brightness figures, from city centres to Kielder and Galloway. It does not know about local lighting, hills, trees, street lamps, the Moon, haze or cloud, and it leaves out villages and industrial sites. A single bright floodlight can matter more than a distant town.

    For measured data, use a sky quality meter, or the satellite-based Light Pollution Map.
